Food delivery website: restaurants, menus, cart and order tracking

A food delivery website has one job: turn hunger into a confirmed order, fast, on a phone, with no confusion about what is available, what it costs, or when it arrives.

The test:
Can a hungry customer find a dish, add it, pay or choose cash on delivery, and see their order move — without calling the restaurant?

What you actually need

  • Menus with real availability. A dish that is sold out must say so before it is added to the cart, not after payment.
  • A cart that shows the full total. Food, delivery fee, total — plainly, before checkout. Surprise fees at the last step kill orders.
  • Order tracking the customer can see. Received, preparing, on the way, delivered. Every status update is one phone call you do not get.
  • An admin for the restaurant. Change the menu, mark items out, see new orders, mark them ready. If the kitchen has to phone a developer to change a price, the system is broken.

Step by step

  1. Write the real menu first, with prices and which items are actually available today.
  2. Set delivery areas and fees before launch, and show them on the menu page, not only at checkout.
  3. Build restaurant, menu, cart and tracking as one flow, and test it on a phone with a real order.
  4. Add the restaurant admin next: menu edits, order list, status changes.
  5. Launch with a small, complete menu. Twenty dishes that can actually be delivered beat a hundred listings with no stock behind them.
  6. After launch, watch where orders drop: menu, cart, or checkout — and fix that step before adding new features.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Letting customers order sold-out dishes. One cancelled dinner costs more trust than a menu that honestly says "sold out".
  • Hiding the delivery fee until checkout. That is the most common reason food carts are abandoned.
  • No order status. Without tracking, every customer phones to ask "where is my food?" — exactly the work the website should remove.
  • Starting from a blank project for a standard system. Restaurants, menus, cart and tracking have been built many times. Rebuilding them burns the budget that should go to food and delivery.
