Inventory and POS in one place: stock control without spreadsheets

A small shop loses money in two quiet ways: selling stock it does not have, and not knowing what actually sells. A spreadsheet updated "when there is time" is always out of date — which means it is never true.

The test:
When a sale happens at the counter, does stock fall by itself, and can you see today's sales, without anyone typing numbers into a sheet?

What you actually need

  • Products with real stock counts. Every product, every variant, one true number. If two places hold the number, they will disagree.
  • A POS that is fast at the counter. Scan or tap, total, pay, done. A slow checkout is a queue, and a queue is lost sales.
  • Stock that moves with sales. Sell one, stock falls by one — automatically, every time, with no manual step to forget.
  • Reports you can act on. What sold today, what is running low, what has not moved in a month. That is how you order, price and clear stock.

Step by step

  1. Count real stock once, properly, and enter that as the starting truth. A system built on guessed stock only automates the guess.
  2. Give every product a price and a low-stock level, so the system can warn you before you run out — not after a customer asks.
  3. Run all counter sales through the POS from day one. Sales outside the system are stock the system cannot see.
  4. Record supplier deliveries as stock in, with the same product names. Different names for the same product split your stock in two.
  5. Check the low-stock and slow-moving reports weekly, and order or discount from those — not from feeling.
  6. Only then add extras: suppliers, multi-branch, or online sales. They do not matter if counter stock is not true.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Two stock truths. A spreadsheet plus a POS plus a notebook means no one knows the real number.
  • Sales outside the system. Every cash sale skipped is stock that looks present but is already gone.
  • No low-stock warning. Finding out you are out of a bestseller from a customer is the most expensive way to find out.
  • Tracking everything, acting on nothing. A report nobody reads weekly is decoration, not control.
