AI chatbot for your business: answer FAQs at 2am without hiring

Every business has the same ten questions: prices, sizes, delivery, opening hours, cash on delivery, returns, location. Owners answer them at midnight, on Messenger, over and over — about ten hours a week, on work a machine should do.

The test:
At 2am, can a customer ask a real question, get a correct answer from your own prices and policies, and leave their details if they want to buy — without anyone awake?

What you actually need

  • Answers trained on YOUR content. Your prices, sizes, delivery rules and policies — not generic answers from the internet. A chatbot that guesses your prices is worse than no chatbot.
  • A clear handoff to a human. When the question is unusual, or the customer is ready to buy, the bot should capture name and number and hand over — not loop forever.
  • A record you can review. Every conversation, logged. That log is how you find the questions you forgot to answer on your website.
  • One place customers already are. Your website, Messenger, or store — not a new app customers must learn.

Step by step

  1. Write down your ten most-asked questions, with the exact correct answers. This list is the chatbot's brain; do not skip it.
  2. Add your prices, delivery areas and fees, payment options and return rule, in plain words.
  3. Set the bot to answer only from that content, and to say when it does not know — then capture the question for you.
  4. Test it with awkward, real questions: wrong sizes, out-of-stock items, delivery to a new area. Fix the answers that fail.
  5. Put it where customers already ask: website first, then Messenger. One channel working beats three half-working.
  6. Review the log weekly. The questions that keep appearing are the pages you need to fix on your site.

Mistakes to avoid

  • A bot that invents prices. One wrong price quoted at 2am is a lost customer and an argument in the morning.
  • No handoff. A bot that never lets a ready buyer reach a human is a wall, not a helper.
  • Training it on generic text. Your business is not every business. Generic answers sound helpful and are wrong in the details that matter.
  • Launching it and never reading the log. The log is the product improving itself. Ignore it and the bot stays dumb.
