Blog and CMS website: posts, categories and moderated comments

A blog CMS website sounds simple until you actually run one. The first post is easy. The tenth post needs categories so readers can find anything. The twentieth needs search, an author page, and a way to stop spam comments from filling the page overnight. A proper blog and CMS setup handles all of that from the start, so publishing stays the easy part.

This guide is for founders, agencies and small teams who publish regularly — company news, guides, product updates, local stories — and are tired of editing pages by hand or pasting posts into a page builder that was never meant for writing. If you publish more than once a month, you need a real content system, not another static page.

The test:
Can a non-technical team member write a post, add it to the right category, preview it, and publish it — without touching code or asking a developer?

What you actually need

  • A real editor, not a text box. Headings, lists, images, links and quotes, saved as structured content — so a post looks right on a phone and on a desktop without manual formatting.
  • Categories and tags that readers use. A handful of clear categories, not twenty overlapping ones. Every post gets one category and a few tags, so archives stay browsable instead of becoming a pile.
  • Moderated comments. Comments appear only after approval. Spam never reaches the page, and genuine readers still get a voice — with a reply thread the author can actually follow.
  • Drafts, scheduling and preview. Write today, publish on Monday. Preview exactly how the post will look before it goes live, and keep drafts private until they are ready.
  • Author accounts with roles. Writers write, editors approve, admins publish. Nobody needs the main admin login to write a post, and nobody can accidentally delete the whole blog.
  • Search that finds old posts. A reader who lands on one guide should find the related ones. Search across titles and content, not just the latest ten posts.

Step by step

  1. Decide your categories before you write: five or six topics your readers actually browse by. Name them in the reader's words, not internal jargon.
  2. Set up author roles first — writer, editor, admin — and give each person the lowest role that lets them do their job.
  3. Write your first three posts in draft, covering three different categories, so the site never launches with one empty category and one overflowing one.
  4. Turn comment moderation on from day one: every comment waits for approval, and approval takes one click from the admin screen.
  5. Add a simple publishing routine: draft, preview on mobile, check the category and tags, schedule or publish. The same steps every time, so nothing ships half-formatted.
  6. Connect the blog to the rest of the site: latest posts on the homepage, related posts at the end of each article, and a working search box in the header.
  7. Review the archive monthly: merge categories with one post in them, retag older posts, and update the posts that still get traffic but carry outdated facts.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Unmoderated comments. Open comments without approval turn into a spam board within weeks, and cleaning it up costs more time than moderation ever did.
  • Too many categories. Twenty categories with one post each help nobody. Start small; split a category only when it clearly holds too much.
  • Publishing straight to live with no draft step. Typos, broken images and wrong categories all slip through when there is no preview. Draft first, always.
  • One shared admin login for all writers. When a post disappears or changes, you need to know who did it. Separate accounts are not bureaucracy — they are the audit trail.
