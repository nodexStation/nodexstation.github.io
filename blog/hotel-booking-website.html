Hotel booking website: rooms, availability and reservations that work

A hotel website fails in one specific way: it shows beautiful rooms, then makes booking so hard that the guest phones, messages, or books somewhere else. The booking flow is the website.

The test:
Can a guest pick dates, see only rooms that are actually free, see the real price, and be confirmed — at midnight, without anyone at reception?

What you actually need

  • Rooms with real prices and photos that match. A room type is a promise: bed, size, amenities, price per night. If the photo is a different room, the guest will complain at check-in.
  • Live availability by date. A room booked for those dates must disappear for those dates. This is the whole point of the system.
  • A reservation record both sides can check. Guest name, dates, room, price, status — confirmed, not "we will call you back".
  • An admin calendar. Which rooms are free, which are booked, and what is coming tomorrow — at a glance.

Step by step

  1. List every room type with its true price, capacity and amenities. Do not list a room you cannot actually sell.
  2. Set your booking rules first: check-in/out times, cancellation rule, and payment or deposit — and show them before booking, not after.
  3. Build search by dates first, then rooms, then price, then confirmation. That order prevents the most common booking errors.
  4. Confirm immediately on screen, with a record the guest can check later.
  5. Give reception a simple calendar and a way to mark a booking checked in, completed or cancelled.
  6. After a month, check the pattern: which room types fill, which dates sit empty, and adjust price or rules from that — not from guesswork.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Booking by message form. A form that says "we will reply" is not booking; it is a queue for missed calls.
  • Availability that is not live. Double bookings are not a small bug for a hotel — they are a guest arriving with nowhere to sleep.
  • Hiding taxes and fees until the last step. Guests who see the real total early book more often and complain less.
  • Photos that do not match the room. The fastest way to a bad review is a guest opening a door to a different room than the one they paid for.
