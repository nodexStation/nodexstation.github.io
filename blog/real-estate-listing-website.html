Real estate website: listings, search and buyer inquiries

A property website fails when a buyer cannot find the right property, or when an inquiry arrives with no property attached and the agent has to ask "which one?" three times. Search and inquiries are the website.

The test:
Can a buyer filter by area, price and type, open a property, see real details and photos, and send an inquiry that names that exact property?

What you actually need

  • Listings with real details. Area, price, size, rooms, type, status — sold, available, or under offer. A listing with a photo and no price is not a listing.
  • Search and filters buyers actually use. Area, price range, property type, bedrooms. Filters that do not match how buyers think are decoration.
  • Inquiries tied to a property. Name, phone, message, and the property it is about — in one record the agent can follow up.
  • An admin who can publish and update fast. Add a listing, mark it sold, change a price. If that takes a developer, listings go stale.

Step by step

  1. Write down your real listings first, with honest prices and statuses. A stale "available" listing wastes every inquiry it attracts.
  2. Set the filters from buyer questions: where, how much, what type, how many rooms.
  3. Build listing pages around photos and the facts buyers ask first: price, area, size, rooms, contact.
  4. Put the inquiry form on the listing page itself, with the property already attached. Do not send buyers to a generic contact page.
  5. Give agents one inquiry list, sorted by property and date, with a way to mark each as contacted, viewing booked, or closed.
  6. Weekly, remove or update sold and stale listings. A site full of sold properties teaches buyers not to trust it.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Listings without prices. "Price on request" on every listing does not create inquiries — it creates bounces.
  • Generic contact forms. An inquiry that does not name the property costs the agent a round of questions and the buyer a day.
  • Stale listings. Nothing damages an agency's trust faster than advertising properties that sold months ago.
  • Photos that do not match. The fastest way to a wasted viewing is a listing photo from a different property or a different year.
