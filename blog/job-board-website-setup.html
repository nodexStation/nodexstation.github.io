Job board website: postings, applications and employer accounts

A job board fails when job seekers cannot find the right job, or when applications arrive as loose emails with no job attached and the employer has to sort them by hand. Postings, applications and employer accounts are the whole system.

The test:
Can an employer post a job, can a seeker find it by role and area, and can they apply in a way that lands in one list tied to that job?

What you actually need

  • Job postings with real details. Role, company, area, salary range if any, type, deadline. A posting with a title and no details gets no serious applicants.
  • Search seekers actually use. Role, area, job type. If seekers cannot filter, they leave — and employers notice the empty applicant list.
  • Applications tied to a job. Applicant, CV or details, and the job they applied for — in one record the employer can review, shortlist or reject.
  • Employer accounts. Employers post, edit and close their own jobs, and see their own applicants — without emailing an admin for every change.

Step by step

  1. Decide your categories first: roles and areas your board will actually serve. A board for "everything" serves no one well.
  2. Build the posting form around what seekers ask: role, company, area, type, salary, deadline, how to apply.
  3. Let employers create an account, post, and edit their jobs themselves. Admin-only posting does not scale past a handful of jobs.
  4. Put the apply step on the job page itself, with the job already attached. Do not send seekers to a generic email address.
  5. Give employers one applicant list per job, with a way to mark each as reviewed, shortlisted or rejected.
  6. Close or expire old postings automatically. A board full of dead jobs teaches seekers not to come back.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Applications by loose email. An application that does not name the job costs the employer sorting time and the seeker a lost chance.
  • Postings without area or type. Seekers filter by those first. Without them, your board is a pile, not a search.
  • Admin-only posting. If every job needs you, the board stops growing the week you get busy.
  • Dead jobs left live. Nothing kills a job board faster than seekers applying to jobs that closed months ago.
