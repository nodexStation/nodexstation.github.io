Pharmacy store online: catalog, prescriptions and stock-enforced orders

An online pharmacy store has one job a regular shop does not: a customer finds the medicine they need, orders only what is genuinely in stock, and — where a prescription is required — the order does not go through without one. Get that right and a local pharmacy serves its whole town from a phone. Get it wrong and you sell medicine you do not have, or you dispense a prescription-only item on a click.

Most small pharmacies still take orders by phone or message: a customer names a medicine, someone checks the shelf, the price is quoted from memory, and the order is written down somewhere. It works until the shelf is empty, the price is wrong, or the message is lost. An online pharmacy store moves that exchange onto the screen: a searchable catalog with real prices and live stock, a prescription upload step for items that need one, and orders blocked the moment stock runs out. This guide is for pharmacy owners who want that loop, without building a pharmacy platform from zero.

What you actually need

  • A searchable catalog with real prices and doses. Medicines listed by name, strength and pack size, with the price the customer actually pays — findable in seconds, not by scrolling a wall of names.
  • Stock that is enforced, not displayed. When the last pack is sold, the item stops being orderable — instantly. An order for medicine you do not have is worse than no order at all.
  • A prescription step for items that need one. Prescription-only medicines ask for a prescription upload before checkout, and the order waits for your check before it is confirmed.
  • An order flow a customer can finish alone. Search, add to cart, upload a prescription if needed, choose delivery or pickup, pay or pay on delivery, get a confirmation. Every phone call the flow needs is a place the order dies.
  • Delivery and pickup that match how your town buys. Home delivery with a time window, or pickup at the counter with a ready time — both with an order status the customer can see.
  • A back office that shows what matters today. Orders waiting on a prescription check, items running low, and today's deliveries.

Step by step

  1. List your catalog honestly: name, strength, pack size, price, and whether it needs a prescription. Start with the medicines people ask for most, not with everything on your shelves.
  2. Mark which items need a prescription and make the upload step part of checkout, before payment. A customer should never pay for an item you cannot legally hand over.
  3. Connect stock to the order button: an item with zero stock cannot be added to a cart, and stock falls the moment an order is confirmed, not when someone remembers to update a sheet.
  4. Set your delivery rule plainly on the site: delivery area, fee, and time window — or pickup only. Say it before checkout, so nobody orders from outside the area you serve.
  5. After the first month, look at the orders: which medicines sell out, which searches return nothing, which orders stall on prescriptions. That is your restock list.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Letting customers order out-of-stock medicine. If stock is checked after the order is placed instead of before the item is added to the cart, you end up calling customers to cancel orders they already paid for. Check first, show only what is available.
  • Treating prescriptions as an afterthought. If a prescription-only item can be bought with no upload step, you have built a shop that breaks its own rules. The prescription step belongs in the flow, not in a note nobody reads.
  • A catalog with no doses or pack sizes. "Paracetamol" is not an order. A customer who cannot tell 500mg from 650mg, or a strip from a box, will order the wrong thing.
