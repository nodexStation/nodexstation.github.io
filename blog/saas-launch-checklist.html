SaaS launch checklist: auth, billing and dashboard before you go live

A SaaS launch fails quietly when the product works but the business around it does not: nobody can sign up safely, nobody can pay, and nobody can see what they paid for. Get those three right first.

The launch test:
Can a stranger sign up, pay, and use the product — and can you see that they did — without you touching anything by hand?

What you actually need

  • Authentication that is boring and safe. Sign up, sign in, password reset, and sessions that actually expire. This is not the place to be clever.
  • Real billing, not a "pay later" note. Plans, a checkout that charges, subscriptions that renew or cancel, and a record of every payment.
  • A dashboard the user understands in ten seconds. What they have, what they can do next, and how to manage their plan. If they have to email you to find out, the dashboard failed.
  • An admin view for you. Users, plans, payments and problems — in one place, with real data.

Step by step

  1. Define one core job your product does, and one plan structure. Two clear plans beat five confusing ones.
  2. Build auth first, with password reset tested end to end. A user locked out of a paid account is a refund waiting to happen.
  3. Connect real billing in test mode, then run a full paid signup yourself: card, charge, access granted, receipt recorded.
  4. Build the dashboard around the core job, not around settings pages. The first screen should show progress, not configuration.
  5. Test cancellation and failed payments. These happen to every SaaS; handling them calmly keeps customers.
  6. Only then add the nice extras: onboarding tours, analytics, integrations. They do not matter if signup and billing do not work.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Launching with manual billing. Taking payments by message and granting access by hand does not scale past a handful of users, and it breaks at night.
  • Storing passwords badly or skipping reset. There is no excuse for either in a product that charges money.
  • A dashboard full of charts and no actions. Users pay for outcomes, not for a wall of graphs.
  • Building billing last. Billing touches auth, access, plans and admin. Bolting it on at the end means rebuilding all four.
