How to sell courses online with real student progress tracking

Selling a course is not hard. Keeping students moving through it is. A pile of videos behind a paywall is not a learning platform — students stall, teachers cannot see where, and refunds follow.

The test:
Can a student buy, start lesson one, pick up where they left off, and can the teacher see exactly how far they got?

What you actually need

  • Courses broken into lessons, in order. Short, titled lessons with a clear next step beat one long video nobody finishes.
  • Enrollment that grants access automatically. Pay, get in. No manual account creation by the teacher at midnight.
  • Progress a student can see. What is done, what is next, how much is left. Progress is motivation.
  • Progress a teacher can see. Who started, who stalled, which lesson loses people. That is how a course gets better.

Step by step

  1. Outline the outcome first: what will a student be able to do at the end? Then work backwards into lessons.
  2. Keep lessons short and single-topic. If a lesson tries to teach three things, split it.
  3. Set the course page to sell the outcome and the structure: lessons, length, what is included — not just a trailer.
  4. Connect enrollment to access: purchase grants the course immediately, and the student lands on lesson one.
  5. Track completion per lesson, and show it to both student and teacher. Do not track "time on page" and call it progress.
  6. After the first cohort, fix the lesson where most students stop. That one lesson is usually the whole retention problem.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Uploading videos and calling it a course. Without order, progress and enrollment, it is a playlist with a price.
  • Manual enrollment. Every manual step is a student waiting, and a sale that cools off.
  • No progress view for the teacher. If you cannot see where students stall, you cannot improve the course.
  • One giant lesson per topic. Long lessons are where students quit. Short, finished lessons are where they continue.
