How salons book appointments online without phone tag

A salon loses bookings in the gaps: the call that comes while a stylist is busy, the message answered hours later, the slot that was promised to two people. Online booking closes those gaps — if it is built around how a salon actually works.

The test:
Can a customer pick a service, pick a person, pick a free time, and be confirmed — at midnight, without anyone at the salon lifting a phone?

What you actually need

  • Services with real durations and prices. A haircut and a colour are not the same length. If the system books both in 30 minutes, the day collapses.
  • Staff calendars that cannot double-book. A slot, once taken, must disappear for that person. This is the whole point of the system.
  • Customer details you can actually use. Name, phone, service, staff, time — and a way to contact them if a slot changes.
  • A staff view of the day. Who is coming, for what, and when — at a glance, without digging through messages.

Step by step

  1. Write down every service with its true duration, price and who can do it. Do not guess durations — time a real appointment.
  2. Set working hours per staff member, including breaks. The system should only offer times someone can actually work.
  3. Let customers choose service, then staff, then time. That order prevents the most common booking errors.
  4. Confirm immediately on screen, and keep a record the customer can check. "We will call you back to confirm" is phone tag with extra steps.
  5. Give staff a simple daily list and a way to mark a booking done, no-show or rescheduled.
  6. After a week, check the pattern: which services fill, which times sit empty, and adjust hours or prices from that — not from guesswork.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Booking by message form. A form that says "we will reply" is not booking; it is a queue for missed calls.
  • One calendar for the whole salon. Two stylists are not interchangeable. Without per-person calendars, double bookings are guaranteed.
  • No durations. If every service takes "one slot", the schedule will overrun every day.
  • Hiding prices until the chair. Customers who know the price before they book show up more often.
